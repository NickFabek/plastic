Interpretability × training efficiency · small-scale experiments

PLASTIC

If trained neurons really are clean abstractions, can we write them from an LLM's description of the data instead of learning them, and then hold them in place while the network trains?

GPT-2-token ClimbMix · 29M-parameter GPT trained from scratch in hand-written CUDA on one RTX 3080

The idea

Neurons as abstractions you can write down

Goodfire's essay Neural networks are already clean argues that a network's own neurons are mostly monosemantic. They are not buckets of unrelated features. Each encodes an abstraction that is coarse when neurons are scarce and sharpens with scale. If that is right, an abstraction is something you could name in advance, bin data into, and turn into weights.

PLASTIC tests that directly. An LLM reads the corpus and proposes abstractions. A second model sorts text into them. Closed-form statistics of each bin (no gradient descent) become a neuron's input and output weights. Then the network trains normally, optionally with a righting force that pulls the written neurons back toward their assigned meaning, the way a keel pulls a heeling boat upright.

Step 4 · the righting force

Holding the written neurons in place

What does it mean for a written neuron to still be there? Interpretability microscopes describe a neuron by what drives it (its strongest activations, at both poles) and by what it does downstream (its causal role in attribution graphs), not by its raw weights. So each run is scored three ways: are its weights near the construction, is it still selective for its concept, and is it still used, meaning that removing it costs loss. The three can come apart.
